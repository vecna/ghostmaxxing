Workshops

Bring your face and curiosity.

Hands-on sessions where we test local face-recognition behaviour together. The goal is to fool machines, not people. The game is the least paint that still works, not the most.

How a session runs

1. The only tool you need is water-based face paint sold for children. A cheap kit is enough. Take more brushes than it comes with: in practice the darkest and lightest colours do most of the work.
2. Open the lab and save your face while it is still clean. That gives you an ID to test against.
3. Paint. Break the lines of the face. In workshops people usually pair up and paint each other.
4. Go back to the webcam. Success is either no face detected at all, or your ID marked "eluded".
5. Second check, no lab needed: any phone app with a face filter. If it asks you to find a face, you did it.
6. Now the actual problem. Heavy paint is easy, and a clown face is not a result anyone can use. Aim for the least paint that still works.
7. Share what worked. Contributions are ranked by how subtle they are, so a look that takes two lines is worth more here than one that takes a mask.
