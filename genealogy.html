Genealogy of Face Surveillance

Which interventions affected earlier systems, where they stopped working, and what is still untested against today’s pipelines.

How to read this

One row per family of face-reading system, oldest first. The heavy segment is the years that family led. Circles on the line are graded entries in the reference archive; the shapes under it are catalogued projects. Each sits at the year it was first shown in public and links to its entry.

Networked readers and wearable readers: no graded reference, no catalogued project.

2001–2009

Face detection

Haar cascades · Viola-Jones

Rectangles of light and dark slid across the frame. Cheap, fast, everywhere, and it only ever looked for contrast.

3 references, 6 projects.

2010–2013

Landmark geometry

Eigenfaces · Fisherfaces · 68-point meshes

The face as a set of distances between named points. The dome never shows you which way it looks.

No reference, 2 projects.

2014–2016

Learned embeddings

DeepFace · FaceNet · ArcFace · deep detectors

Systems stop measuring features and start learning them: every face becomes a vector in a space nobody designed by hand. The same networks read people, objects and number plates.

13 references, 13 projects.

2017–2020

Depth & near-infrared

Dot projectors · NIR sensors · liveness

The face as 3D geometry, read in the dark, with a liveness check a photograph cannot pass. Interventions at this layer address the sensor, not the matcher.

4 references, 12 projects.

2020–now

Networked readers

Pole cameras · shared watchlists

Fixed cameras stop being cameras and become one queryable index. The reading is unchanged; the reach is not.

No reference, no project.

2020–now

Wearable readers

Pervert glasses · body-worn

The lens moves to eye level and stops looking like a lens. A different technology from the pole, and a different problem: you cannot learn to avoid a camera you cannot identify.

No reference, no project.

Dates comes from first web appearance. A reference is a documented result under stated conditions; the conditions should be in the archive entry, but reproduceability isn't assured. A project is a catalogued product, prototype, artwork or practice. Those products should be tested and ranked. Ghostmaxxing wants to be an hub for such evaluations. Currently considered are 20 references, 7 of them with code or artefacts published. 33 projects, 15 of them commercial. If you know any project that should be referenced here, please get in touch.
