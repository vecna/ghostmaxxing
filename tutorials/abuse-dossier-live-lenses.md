# The two live lenses: documented abuse, September 2026

Research file for the homepage camera facts, the genealogy canopy and the
reporting node. Compiled 6 September 2026.

The genealogy ends with two lenses that carry no documented interruption: the
**pole-mounted plate reader** and the **camera worn on a face**. This file
collects what those two are documented to have done, with a source for each
that opens without a paywall and without an account.

House rules applied. Every row is a specific system, in a stated place, at a
stated time, from a named source: the claims ladder calls that documented, not
inferred. Where the original reporting sits behind a wall, the row links to
coverage that does not, and the paywalled original is named in the notes so
credit stays where it belongs.

## Pole-mounted readers (Flock and the ALPR networks)

| # | What happened | Where and when | Why it belongs here | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A sheriff's office searched **83,345 cameras across 6,809 networks** for a woman who had ended her own pregnancy. The search was logged as a missing-person welfare check; the case file said "had an abortion, search for female". The DA had already said she could not be charged. | Texas, May 2025 | The clearest case of a network built for stolen cars being turned on a body. The cover story was in the log field the vendor points to as accountability. | [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/10/flock-safety-and-texas-sheriff-claimed-license-plate-search-was-missing-person-it) |
| 2 | An officer ran **2,048 searches** on his ex-girlfriend's two cars in five months. **241** of them were run while a protection order against him was active. Arrested on ten counts. | Shively, Kentucky, arrested 26 Aug 2026 | Not a loophole. The system did exactly what it is sold to do, for five months, before anyone noticed. | [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51616576/police-officer-arrested-after-tracking-ex-girlfriend-on-flock-camera-system-over-2000-times-authorities-say) |
| 3 | An officer searched for his ex-girlfriend and people connected to her **more than 10,000 times**. A Florida deputy searched for his estranged wife's car **more than 700 times**. A Georgia police chief was arrested for stalking a former partner through the readers. | US, 2023-2026 | Three entries from one database. The pattern is the finding, not the individual. | [Institute for Justice](https://ij.org/the-ij-database-of-alpr-abuse/) |
| 4 | Police in a sanctuary state let federal immigration agents run **more than 4,000 searches** through a network of **40,000 cameras** in 5,000 communities, with reasons typed as "ICE", "illegal immigration", "ICE WARRANT". State law forbids using this data for immigration enforcement. | Danville, Illinois, records covering Jun 2024 to May 2025 | The audit trail was not the safeguard. It was the evidence, and only because a journalist filed a records request. | [Reason](https://reason.com/2025/05/29/illinois-cops-gave-ice-access-to-more-than-5000-surveillance-cameras-nationwide/) |
| 5 | An innocent couple was pulled over **at gunpoint** with their six-week-old baby in the back seat after a misread digit. Grandparents were held at gunpoint with a three-year-old watching after an "O" was read as a "0". A passenger spent nearly a month in jail over a wrong "vehicle signature". In one city **71% of 1,427 alerts** carried an incorrect plate read. | Arkansas Feb 2026, Tennessee Jun 2024, San Diego Nov 2025, Roseville 2023 | The failure mode is not only the machine being used wrongly. It is the machine being wrong and believed anyway. | [Institute for Justice](https://ij.org/dozens-of-innocent-motorists-have-been-pulled-over-detained-at-gunpoint-or-jailed-due-to-ai-license-plate-camera-errors/) |
| 6 | The vendor ran a webinar teaching police to watch **protest crowds** in real time, listing the No Kings demonstrations beside parades and bike races: plate readers, drones, gunshot sensors and live video on one screen. **4,800 cities** run the software. "I want to make sure I have as many tools to make myself dangerous if and when I need it." | US, reported 3 Sep 2026 | The public pitch is stolen cars. The sales pitch is crowd control. | [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/flock-taught-cops-how-to-surveil-no-kings-protesters/) |
| 7 | Police search URLs, including **plates, case numbers and the stated reason for the search**, were indexed by DuckDuckGo and Bing. Found by volunteers browsing search results. A public tally of disclosed searches had by then reached roughly **184 million** searches over **4.4 million** plates. | Discovered May 2026, reported Jun 2026 | The most sensitive field in the system, the reason, leaked through the most ordinary channel there is. | [Gadget Review](https://www.gadgetreview.com/police-license-plate-searches-leak-into-public-search-results-through-flock-safety-urls) |
| 8 | The company sent a **cease and desist** to the volunteer running the open-source map of its cameras, claiming trademark dilution. He refused, with EFF representing him, and kept mapping. | Feb 2025 | A surveillance network arguing that being mapped is the violation. | [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/02/anti-surveillance-mapmaker-refuses-flock-safetys-cease-and-desist-demand) |
| 9 | People began physically dismantling the cameras across at least five states. The volunteer map counted roughly **80,000** in service nationally. | US, reported Feb 2026 | Documented, and worth stating plainly: this is what happens when a deployment outruns its consent. | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/americans-are-destroying-flock-surveillance-cameras) |
| 10 | A state transport department **revoked the permits** for readers on state right-of-way and removals began. Pasco County alone pulled 70; Tampa 17. | Florida, memo 1 Sep 2026 | The first case in this file where the challenge was administrative and it worked. | [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/transportation/2026-09-02/law-enforcement-agencies-remove-flock-cameras-florida-roads) |

## Cameras worn on a face (smart glasses)

| # | What happened | Where and when | Why it belongs here | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Two students wired a face search engine and public-records lookups to a pair of Ray-Bans: look at a stranger, get **name, home address, phone number, relatives**, on the phone in your pocket. They tested it on people in public who had not consented, and did not release the code. | Harvard, Oct 2024 | The demo that ended the "no face recognition in these glasses" argument. It was never about what the manufacturer shipped. | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/02/ray-ban-meta-facial-recognition-terminator-vision-for-doxxing) |
| 12 | The **same two people** dropped out and launched always-on recording glasses at **$249**, with microphones that run continuously and **no external indicator**. On consent, one of them said the company "trusts our users to get consent". | Aug 2025 | The people who proved the harm now sell the product, and outsourced the ethics to the buyer. | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/20/harvard-dropouts-to-launch-always-on-ai-smart-glasses-that-listen-and-record-every-conversation) |
| 13 | Researchers analysed **350 Instagram videos** of women filmed in public through smart glasses, posted between Sep 2023 and Mar 2026. They found the **more covert the recording modality, the more severe the harassment**; **93.3%** of posts left comments open, and women reported being tracked to their workplaces afterwards. | University of Sydney, preprint Aug 2026 | The harm is measured, not asserted, and it scales with invisibility. That is the whole argument for a test bench. | [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-pov-instagram-pickup-artists/) |
| 14 | Footage from the glasses is reviewed by **human annotators**. Workers at a Nairobi subcontractor described seeing **bathroom visits, people undressing, sex, pornography and bank cards**, and said the automatic face blurring frequently failed. More than 30 workers were interviewed; the company took two months to answer and did not address the questions. | Nairobi, reported Mar 2026 by Swedish papers | The wearer consented to a product. Everyone in frame consented to nothing, and the footage left the phone. | [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/05/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-privacy-risks/) |
| 15 | The manufacturer **remotely disabled the cameras on around 7,000 pairs** whose owners had tampered with the recording indicator light. | Sep 2026 | Seven thousand people cared enough about hiding the light to void their hardware. | [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/09/meta-bricks-ray-ban-glasses-stop-covert-filming.html) |
| 16 | People are now trying to defeat the glasses in public: singing copyrighted songs at wearers, running scanner apps that detect nearby devices, blocking the indicator. A lawyer notes the copyright trick does not work because the person being filmed owns nothing in the recording. | Reported Aug 2026 | Improvised countermeasures, in the wild, with no test bench. This is the reason Ghostmaxxing exists. | [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/14/people-stop-filming-meta-pervert-glasses-privacy/) |
| 17 | The platform began removing the genre of videos filmed covertly on the glasses, after they became a recognised category. | Jul 2026 | Moderation arriving after three years of the format is the definition of a countermeasure that is too late. | [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/28/ray-ban-meta-pervert-glasses-secret-videos-women/) |
| 18 | Homeland Security wants **its own smart glasses** for immigration agents: video capture compared against biometric databases, **facial recognition and gait analysis**, identifying people "in real time during interactions", with prototypes targeted for Sep 2027. Agents in **six states** were already found using consumer smart glasses. | Budget documents, reported Apr 2026 | The consumer object and the state object converge. Gait analysis also puts makeup outside the threat model. | [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/wearables/homeland-security-reportedly-wants-to-develop-smart-glasses-for-ice-093449347.html) |

## Synthesis for camera-facts.json

The homepage field has six camera types. Four are eras the genealogy marks as
interrupted; two are the lenses still reading. The rewrite gives those two
groups different jobs.

**The four spent eras answer "what changed".** Each fact names what that
generation could actually do, then what one of the two live lenses does now.
Clicking an old camera should leave you with the size of the escalation, not a
nostalgia fact.

- `bullet` (Haar cascades, 2001-2009): one frame, one machine, where it was
  pointed. Now: one query across 80,000 cameras; every drive-past indexed for
  4,800 cities.
- `dome` (landmark geometry, 2010-2013): needed a cooperative, lit, frontal
  face, and hid its aim. Now: the same measurements taken by a stranger at eye
  level, from a device that hides that a camera exists.
- `cube` (CNN embeddings, 2014-2016): turned a face into a vector but reached
  one house. Now: reach is the product, and the leak is a police search reason
  in Bing rather than a default password.
- `pill` (depth and near-infrared, 2017-2020): beat the photograph and the
  dark, but you had to step in front of it. Now: nothing to step in front of.

**The two live lenses answer "how is it abused".** Each fact is one documented
case with a link out, drawn from the tables above. Seven for `street`, six for
`glasses`, so repeat clicks keep paying.

Notes on the file itself:

- Facts run 117 to 173 characters, against 94 to 130 in the previous version.
  Checked at 1280px: the longest wraps to four lines inside the half-panel,
  the typical one to three. Nothing overflows.
- Em dashes are gone, per `brand-voice.md`. The previous file used them
  throughout, which the publication checklist should have caught.
- External links open in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, which
  `pages-js/index.js` already does for every fact link, and get the ↗ marker.
- Every claim is past tense, specific and attributed, which is what the claims
  ladder requires before a consequential statement can be published as fact.

Maintenance: the abuse cases are dated and will age. The two aggregate
databases (IJ, DeFlock) age more slowly and are the right thing to re-check
before a launch. Anything in this file older than a year should be re-verified
or replaced rather than quietly left in place.
