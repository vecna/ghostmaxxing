Optional guide · Face Brush

Sketch digitally. Test physically.

Paint a face-anchored pattern in the Lab, observe the local matcher, then use the digital state as a proposal for a real makeup experiment.

Face Brush is an authoring instrument. It can help locate a promising intervention, but it cannot predict how pigment, skin, light, camera processing, or another model will reproduce the digital composite.

The workflow

Use the screen as a sketchbook, not an oracle.

1 · BaselineSave a clean face under controlled conditions.
2 · PaintDraw a small intervention around selected facial regions.
3 · ObserveWatch the composite distance over several updates.
4 · ReproduceTranslate the promising digital pattern into real makeup.
5 · RetestRun the physically painted face as a new experiment.

The digital and physical results are separate evidence. Preserve that separation in notes, screenshots, filenames, and claims.

Minimum procedure

Paint with a baseline active.

01

Save the clean face

Use the same camera, pose, and light you intend to keep during the digital sketch.

02

Activate Brush

Select Face Brush from the Ghostyles. The control bar exposes colour, size, opacity, Undo, Clear, Copy state, and Bake Ghostyle.

03

Reduce the brush size

Start with marks that remain local to a feature. Work around eyes, brows, cheeks, the bridge and sides of the nose, and the mouth rather than hiding the whole face under three broad strokes.

04

Hold and paint

The plugin stores samples relative to nearby 68-point face-api landmarks and an eye-based frame. Marks follow translation, in-plane rotation, and scale while the face remains detectable.

05

Observe a series

Wait for several composite analyses after each small change. Keep a note of the baseline, threshold, brush settings, and regions changed.

06

Save a proposal

Copy state preserves the stroke data. Bake Ghostyle downloads a standalone plugin proposal. Neither action validates a physical result.

The current plugin and browser harness expose enough information to automate this accurately. Playwright can read window.gstmxx.getLastResult(), obtain the 68 landmark positions, set a smaller brush size, and generate pointer paths derived from those points. The 3D mesh is available too, but the current Brush stores its anchors against the 2D face-api landmarks, so the 68-point geometry is the correct source for this specific capture.

Physical translation

Make the second experiment explicit.

  • Photograph or record the digital proposal before clearing it.
  • Write down colour, approximate width, opacity, side of the face, and relationship to visible features.
  • Use skin-safe materials and an appropriate removal method.
  • Reproduce the pattern as closely as practical, then begin a new run with the real painted face.
  • Compare digital and physical observations without implying that one caused or guaranteed the other.

A digitally opaque cyan mark and a layer of real cosmetic pigment have different optics. The camera, light, compression, skin texture, and material finish can change the machine-readable image.

Limits

Do not optimise for a label alone.

Broad occlusion can make a detector lose the face, but it may also produce an impractical or misleading design. A useful authoring session records where the pattern sits, how visible it is, whether the face remains trackable, and how stable the result is across frames.

The Lab is not a liveness detector and the Brush is not an adversarial optimisation algorithm. It lets a person explore a local response through direct manipulation.
